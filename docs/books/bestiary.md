# The Living Bestiary

**Library:** FSM_Dungeon Fantasy Library  
**LOD:** 1 — general creature and ecology framework  
**Status:** authored framework with original illustrative entries

## Purpose

A bestiary should describe life in a world, not merely provide a menu of opponents. Creatures occupy habitats, consume resources, reproduce, migrate, compete, cooperate, adapt, and leave evidence. Their relationships with people may include danger, avoidance, domestication, stewardship, trade, reverence, and mutual dependence.

SURPS owns creature ontology, capabilities, motivations, behavior, and rules-facing resolution. This book owns reusable setting descriptions and ecological context. FSM_Dungeon owns underground environmental structure and spatial facts.

## Creature record

A complete entry can grow through these fields:
- **Identity:** stable entry ID, names, synonyms, classifications, and provenance.
- **Appearance:** observable form, variation, age and seasonal differences.
- **Range:** habitat, territory, depth/elevation, migration, and distribution confidence.
- **Needs:** food, water, shelter, temperature, nesting, and other dependencies.
- **Lifecycle:** development, reproduction, lifespan, and seasonal behavior.
- **Social pattern:** solitary, pair-bonded, pack, herd, colony, swarm, or context-dependent.
- **Ecological role:** prey, predator, scavenger, pollinator, decomposer, engineer, parasite, competitor, or symbiont.
- **Signs:** tracks, calls, scent, nests, feeding traces, shed material, and other evidence.
- **Risks:** defensive behavior, toxins, disease, environmental hazards, and uncertainty.
- **Relationships:** other species, settlements, factions, and individual histories.
- **Knowledge:** what ordinary people know, what specialists infer, and what remains disputed.
- **Story use:** hooks and situations without prescribing a required outcome.

## Ecological placement

A generator or author should be able to explain why a creature is present:
- the habitat satisfies its needs;
- prey or food sources are available;
- a migration or seasonal event brought it here;
- a den, nest, resource, or young are being defended;
- an environmental change displaced it;
- a person or organization introduced, bred, summoned, transported, or disturbed it.

Do not place every creature in every suitable-looking chamber. Account for territory, carrying capacity, competition, access, shelter, and the creature's ability to reach the location.

## Original illustrative entries

These entries are deliberately compact. They are original setting seeds, not established canon for every Workshop world.

### Gloamback

A broad-backed cave grazer with layered mineral-rich plates and sensitive whisker fans. It browses fungal shelves and soft mineral growth along damp cavern margins. Adults usually avoid conflict; a cornered animal can crush brittle structures while trying to escape.

- **Habitat:** humid limestone and mixed-rock caverns with stable fungal growth.
- **Signs:** broad paired impressions, scraped fungal shelves, mineral dust, low resonant calls.
- **Ecological role:** grazer and disperser of fungal spores.
- **Story potential:** a settlement's new drainage works have dried the grazer's feeding ground, pushing a herd toward cultivated root beds.

### Glasswing Skirrer

A small nocturnal glider whose translucent wing membranes catch local light. It nests in warm fissures and feeds on insects drawn to mineral seepage and lantern-lit routes.

- **Habitat:** sheltered vertical caverns and warm cracks near insect concentrations.
- **Signs:** fine shed membranes, clicking calls, clusters of pale eggshell.
- **Ecological role:** insect predator and prey for larger cave hunters.
- **Story potential:** a sudden absence may indicate changed airflow, a predator, or a disturbance deeper in the fissure.

### Embermole

A heat-tolerant burrower that follows warm subterranean gradients and feeds on roots, insects, and mineral-associated organisms. It is not a creature made of fire; its name comes from the faint warmth of its nesting chambers.

- **Habitat:** geothermally warmed soil and fractured rock near stable heat sources.
- **Signs:** warm spoil, branching shallow tunnels, root damage.
- **Ecological role:** soil and sediment disturbance; prey for larger hunters.
- **Story potential:** burrowing redirects seepage toward an old retaining wall. The animal is a clue to changing subsurface conditions, not necessarily the cause of a disaster.

## Bestiary and dungeon composition

A bestiary entry can be referenced by:
- a habitat or region;
- a population or territory;
- a den, nest, burrow, lair, or migration route;
- a specific observed individual;
- a historical account or disputed sighting;
- a prepared or emergent encounter in StoryBuilder.

The environment must not silently decide creature intent. Dungeon can state that a den occupies a chamber and that a route is flooded; SURPS determines the creature's behavior under its ontology and rules; StoryBuilder records what the campaign's participants encounter and what actually follows.

## Encounter design

An encounter need not be combat. Consider:
- evidence and tracking;
- avoidance, concealment, and safe passage;
- rescue, care, or relocation;
- negotiation where the creature is intelligent;
- competition over a resource;
- environmental danger caused by animal activity;
- combat as one possible outcome with consequences.

Record the situation, not a mandatory script. Provide alternatives and let player decisions matter.

## LOD progression

- **LOD 0:** a creature or phenomenon is rumored to exist.
- **LOD 1:** broad description, range, ecology, and common signs.
- **LOD 2:** regional population, specific habitat, den/lair topology, local relationships, and encounter context.
- **LOD 3+:** a named individual, unique lineage, persistent population, or campaign-specific lair with recorded history.

## Limits

No statistics, combat formulae, or behavioral engine are defined here. Those belong to SURPS and runtime providers. Illustrative entries are proposals until adopted into a named setting.
