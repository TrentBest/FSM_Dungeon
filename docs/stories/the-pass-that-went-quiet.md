# The Pass That Went Quiet

**Campaign:** The Broken Star  
**Type:** opening adventure / GM preparation packet  
**Provisional adventure ID:** `adventure.broken-star.pass-that-went-quiet`  
**LOD:** 2 — regional playable situation  
**Edition:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** prepared possibilities; no outcome is canon until played and accepted in StoryBuilder

## Adventure purpose

The characters are asked to find a missing courier and restore reliable communication across a mountain pass. The opening problem is local and legible. It introduces the region's people, routes, evidence, and competing interests without requiring the characters to understand the Broken Star or accept a world-saving quest.

The adventure is designed for flexible system-neutral play. It defines no character statistics, combat rules, or mandatory encounter outcomes. SURPS supplies roleplaying ontology and resolution; StoryBuilder records campaign choices and events.

## Starting situation

A courier named **Mara Venn** left the Passwardens' wayhouse carrying a sealed route report. She was expected at the next watch station before dusk. No confirmation arrived. A second traveler reports an unfamiliar light beyond the ridge and animals moving downhill through a normally quiet drainage.

The Passwardens want the courier found and the route reopened. The Deep Measure wants the route inspected before anyone declares it safe. A Lantern Archive factor wants any recovered report preserved intact. These interests may overlap, conflict, or be replaced by campaign-specific factions.

### What is actually happening — GM preparation

Choose one explanation, combine compatible elements, or leave the truth unresolved until play reveals enough evidence:

1. **Environmental shift:** a new fracture has altered airflow and water movement through the old vent. Animals are relocating; the pass is not necessarily under attack.
2. **Deliberate closure:** someone disabled the signal station to prevent traffic from reaching a nearby excavation.
3. **Protective choice:** Mara discovered an injured witness or displaced creature and chose to hide them until the route could be approached safely.
4. **Draconic territory:** an uncommon drake or wyrm has changed its route after disturbance. It is defending a nesting or feeding area, not hunting travelers for sport.
5. **False pattern:** several unrelated events have been interpreted as one threat. The characters may discover that the light, animal movement, and missing courier have different causes.

Do not reveal this section to players. Preserve unused explanations as discarded or unresolved preparation, not as facts.

## Cast

These are functional roles. Give them campaign-specific identities, voices, relationships, and motives before play.

| Role | Immediate goal | Complication |
|---|---|---|
| Wayhouse keeper | Find Mara and keep travelers calm | Fears losing the pass contract |
| Passwarden scout | Establish whether the route is traversable | Has incomplete information and limited supplies |
| Deep Measure surveyor | Inspect the old vent and recent ground movement | Wants access others may deny |
| Archive factor | Recover the sealed report intact | May conceal who commissioned the report |
| Mara Venn, courier | Protect the information and/or a person she encountered | Her priorities may differ from the party's assumptions |

Avoid assigning every faction representative the same opinion as their organization. Individual disagreement is useful.

## Scene graph

The scene graph is a set of available situations, not a prescribed order.

### Scene A — The Wayhouse

**Purpose:** establish the request, local relationships, and the party's first choices.

Available information:
- Mara's departure time and intended route;
- a rough route sketch with an older service path marked;
- conflicting accounts of the light beyond the ridge;
- a recent increase in animal sightings;
- a dispute over who may authorize reopening the pass.

Possible approaches:
- interview witnesses separately;
- inspect the route sketch and compare it with local knowledge;
- negotiate access to survey equipment;
- leave immediately with limited information;
- ask who benefits if the pass remains closed.

**Transition:** any useful lead can point toward the switchback, the watch station, or the old vent.

### Scene B — The Broken Switchback

**Purpose:** make the changed environment observable.

Evidence options:
- fresh rockfall with more than one plausible cause;
- tracks from multiple species moving downhill;
- a damaged marker that may have been moved deliberately;
- water emerging from a crack that was previously dry;
- a safe route that is slower than the exposed direct route.

Possible resolutions:
- clear or bypass the route;
- mark a hazard and return for equipment;
- follow tracks toward the drainage;
- investigate the rockfall before moving it;
- seek another route through the old service path.

Consequences should reflect the chosen method, time spent, and evidence preserved or disturbed.

### Scene C — The Quiet Watch

**Purpose:** discover why communication stopped.

The signal post is unoccupied. Its condition should tell a story:
- the signal mechanism may be disabled, removed, or still functional;
- provisions may indicate an orderly departure or a hurried evacuation;
- a warning may be genuine, misleading, or intended for a particular recipient;
- recent repairs may reveal who last maintained the post.

Do not make every clue point to one answer. Let players form and test hypotheses.

### Scene D — The Old Vent

**Purpose:** connect the pass to the deeper environment.

The vent is a natural passage modified by earlier builders. Airflow and seepage have changed. Its topology, hazards, and construction history are Dungeon-owned environmental facts. Any inhabitants have their own agency and must not be reduced to environmental props.

Possible discoveries:
- a blocked side passage with signs of recent use;
- an older inscription whose translation is uncertain;
- a warm nesting shelf or abandoned den;
- evidence of mineral or water movement;
- a route that connects to a larger ruin or underground network.

The vent can be bypassed. Entering it is a choice, not a required gate.

### Scene E — Mara's Decision

**Purpose:** resolve the immediate human problem without forcing a single moral answer.

Mara may be found at the watch station, in the vent, sheltering elsewhere, or already beyond the pass. She has a reason for her actions that can be understood even if the party disagrees.

She may possess:
- the sealed route report;
- evidence of an environmental change;
- the location of a displaced person or creature;
- a warning that implicates a local interest;
- only a partial account, with the most important fact still uncertain.

The party decides what to do with Mara, the report, and the route. Returning the report intact, sharing its contents, withholding it, or challenging its authority should produce different social consequences.

## Quests and objectives

### Quest 1 — Find the Courier

**Primary objective:** establish Mara's last known location and determine her present condition.

Possible supporting objectives:
- interview witnesses;
- reconstruct the route;
- inspect the signal station;
- follow reliable physical evidence;
- identify contradictory testimony.

Possible outcomes:
- Mara is recovered and returns voluntarily;
- Mara is found but refuses to return until a concern is addressed;
- the party recovers evidence of her fate but not Mara;
- the search reveals that the original request concealed a second objective.

### Quest 2 — Reopen or Secure the Pass

**Primary objective:** provide a defensible recommendation about access.

Possible supporting objectives:
- identify the immediate hazards;
- establish a viable alternate route;
- restore or replace the signal;
- negotiate temporary access restrictions;
- identify who will maintain the route after the party leaves.

Possible outcomes:
- pass reopened with conditions;
- pass remains closed pending specialist inspection;
- alternate route established;
- competing groups take control of the route;
- the party exposes that the closure was used to conceal another activity.

Quest completion is not binary. StoryBuilder should record objective state, evidence, decisions, and accepted events separately.

## Clue handling

Use a clue record with:
- stable clue ID;
- source and location;
- observable description;
- possible interpretations;
- confidence and provenance;
- who has discovered it;
- who knows it in-world;
- whether it has been shared, concealed, altered, or challenged.

A clue is not a conclusion. The GM may prepare likely interpretations, but players can form alternatives. If a roll or action fails, prefer changed costs, lost time, exposure, or partial information over making the entire adventure impossible to continue.

## Optional modules

Attach only what serves the campaign:

- **Bestiary module:** the animal movement is caused by a habitat shift, predator, nesting cycle, or human disturbance. Use creature entries as ecological context, not mandatory combat encounters.
- **Elf storyline module:** an elf archivist recognizes a route notation connected to *The Archive Beneath the Roots*. Their interpretation is a claim with provenance, not automatic truth.
- **Dragon module:** a draconic presence has changed its territory. The party may negotiate, avoid, observe, protect, or confront it. The creature need not appear directly.
- **Ruins module:** the old vent or signal station is part of a larger abandoned complex. Its past use and present occupants are discoverable, not pre-explained.
- **Faction module:** one faction uses the emergency to secure exclusive access. Its members may disagree about the method.

## Consequence ledger

At preparation time, define pressures rather than fixed outcomes:

| Pressure | If ignored | If addressed constructively | If handled coercively |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unsafe route | Further incidents and loss of trust | Shared inspection and conditional access | Short-term opening, possible future blame |
| Missing report | Rumors and competing claims | Evidence-based decisions | Information control and resentment |
| Displaced wildlife | More encounters near settlements | Habitat protection or managed relocation | Immediate clearance, longer-term ecological cost |
| Faction rivalry | Parallel investigations and distrust | Shared access to findings | Retaliation or monopolized access |

The actual consequence is determined by campaign events, not by this table alone.

## End states and next adventures

The opening adventure can lead into several arcs:
- **The Shattered Reach:** trace the environmental changes toward a fragment site.
- **The Archive Beneath the Roots:** follow disputed records and elven memory traditions.
- **The Mountain That Answers:** investigate the vent network and deeper geological or constructed systems.
- **The Price of the Pass:** negotiate trade, safety, access, and resource claims.
- **The Last Light:** return to the larger question of what the Broken Star is and what should happen to it.

The party may pursue none of these. StoryBuilder should preserve the actual next direction chosen by participants.

## StoryBuilder implementation notes

Represent this packet as one Adventure with stable IDs for its quests, scenes, cast roles, clues, and possible outcomes. Attach exact sourcebook revisions when the campaign is assembled. Store the five explanations under GM-only preparation until the GM chooses what is true or leaves them unresolved.

Participant declarations remain proposals. Evaluated results and GM rulings remain distinct. Only accepted consequences become campaign events. A later sourcebook revision must not rewrite this adventure's pinned references or the events already recorded in play.

## Canon and revision policy

This packet is a reusable preparation asset, not a published account of events. Each campaign can adapt names, factions, explanations, and scene order. Record those adaptations in the campaign. Do not alter the shared sourcebooks to make one campaign's version appear universal.
