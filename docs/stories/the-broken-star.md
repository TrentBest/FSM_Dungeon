# The Broken Star

**Type:** reusable fantasy campaign seed  
**Status:** proposal; no world canon established  
**Primary library:** FSM_Dungeon Fantasy Library  
**Campaign authoring:** StoryBuilder  
**Rules and ontology:** SURPS

## One-sentence premise

A fallen celestial object has fractured across a remote mountain range, and its arrival has begun changing the behavior of old places, creatures, weather, and people—while several communities disagree about whether it is a danger, a resource, a message, or a living thing.

## The situation before the players arrive

The region is not waiting for heroes. It already has:
- mountain settlements dependent on difficult passes and seasonal water;
- forest communities whose routes and boundaries predate current maps;
- mining and trading interests seeking rare materials;
- local creatures whose habitats are changing;
- old ruins and underground passages with their own histories;
- competing institutions attempting to interpret the fragments.

The Broken Star is a proposed campaign event. Its true nature is intentionally undecided at campaign creation. The GM can select, combine, or replace explanations before play, and can preserve uncertainty during play.

## Campaign questions

- What actually fell, and what evidence can establish it?
- Why are nearby environments changing?
- Who has a legitimate claim to the fragments?
- What happens if the fragments are moved, divided, studied, destroyed, or left alone?
- Which old histories become relevant?
- Can the communities cooperate without agreeing on what the Star means?

## Factions and interests

These are role prompts, not fully authored organizations:
- **Passwardens:** keep routes open and protect travelers; fear that panic will close the passes.
- **The Greenward Compact:** forest communities seeking to protect watersheds and migration routes.
- **The Deep Measure:** miners, surveyors, and engineers who want safe access and reliable evidence.
- **The Lantern Archive:** historians and record keepers who disagree over who should control discoveries.
- **The Ashen Choir:** a movement that interprets the fall as a warning; its members do not all agree on what should be done.

Each faction needs named leaders, internal disagreement, resources, relationships, and an ordinary civilian constituency before it becomes campaign canon.

## Opening adventure: The Pass That Went Quiet

A courier fails to arrive from a mountain crossing. The last reliable report mentions an unfamiliar light beyond the ridge and animals moving downhill. The party is asked to locate the courier and reopen communication—not to save the world on day one.

Potential scenes:
1. **The Wayhouse:** interview travelers, inspect the last route record, choose supplies and companions.
2. **The Broken Switchback:** discover fresh rockfall and signs that several different creatures crossed the road.
3. **The Quiet Watch:** find an abandoned signal post and evidence of a deliberate warning.
4. **The Old Vent:** enter a natural/constructed passage whose airflow and water behavior have changed.
5. **The Courier's Choice:** discover that the courier survived but chose to protect a witness or carry evidence elsewhere.

Potential quest objectives:
- establish the courier's last known location;
- identify a safe route through the changed pass;
- determine whether the signal post was abandoned, disabled, or repurposed;
- decide what information to return and to whom.

None of these outcomes is mandatory. Player choices may create a different adventure.

## Adventure arcs that can follow

- **The Shattered Reach:** investigate a fragment site and the changes in its surrounding ecology.
- **The Archive Beneath the Roots:** connect the discovery to the elven storyline and disputed history.
- **The Mountain That Answers:** discover whether a deep geological or constructed system is reacting to the Star.
- **The Price of the Pass:** resolve competing claims over access, safety, trade, and resource extraction.
- **The Last Light:** decide the fate of the fragments after enough evidence has accumulated.

## StoryBuilder data model mapping

When authored in StoryBuilder, use the existing story domain for characters, places, scenes, story events, timeline records, claims, sources, observer knowledge, manuscripts, and publication editions. The campaign-builder target should add typed campaign references for:
- pinned sourcebook editions and entry IDs;
- campaign premise and starting state;
- adventures, quests, objectives, clues, and contingencies;
- encounter definitions that reference SURPS resolution rather than reimplementing it;
- sessions, participant declarations, GM interventions, rulings, and accepted consequences.

The current StoryBuilder README describes campaign records and participant-choice proposals as implemented, while full adventure/quest models, sourcebook pinning, live GM dashboard, session lifecycle, and typed rules-evaluation orchestration remain target work. This seed is content ready for authoring; it is not evidence that those campaign features already exist.

## Canon policy

Everything in this file is a proposal. A campaign chooses what becomes true in its version of the setting. Alternative explanations can remain in planning records. Once play establishes events, StoryBuilder should preserve the actual sequence and consequences rather than retrofitting player choices to match a prewritten ending.
